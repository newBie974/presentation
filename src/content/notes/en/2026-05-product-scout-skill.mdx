## The real problem when you're hunting your next idea

Every indie hacker knows the moment: the current project settles into cruise mode, and the question comes back — *"what do I build next?"*

The usual options:

- **ChatGPT "give me 10 SaaS ideas"** → you get 10 generic B2B dashboards nobody will pay for
- **Reddit / Indie Hackers** → four hours scrolling US-centric posts, and you come out feeling like you've seen everything except anything actionable
- **Twitter / TikTok build-in-public** → inspiration, never a framed niche

What's missing is a **dedicated product consultant** who:

1. Does the field research for you
2. Produces ideas fitted to your profile and your geography
3. Scores each one honestly (not a cheerleader)
4. Hands you a plan you can execute

I'd have paid €500 for that. So I built it with Claude instead.

## The solution: Claude Skills

Anthropic recently shipped [Claude Skills](https://www.anthropic.com/news/agent-skills) — a system where you turn Claude into a specialised tool with a `SKILL.md` file dropped into `~/.claude/skills/<name>/`.

In practice, a skill is:

- **A structured system prompt** defining the role, the tone, the process, the output format
- **Persistent**: you don't re-paste it every conversation, you invoke it with `/skill-name`
- **Composable**: Claude triggers it automatically when your request matches, or you force it

It's the difference between asking a consultant "give me some ideas" (vague) and handing them **a structured brief they've already internalised** (precise).

## My skill: `product-scout`

I built a skill called `product-scout`. Its mission:

> Generate 10 digital product ideas worth shipping in a given niche, with real web research, scoring out of 100, and a full plan: user need, MVP, competitors, dev time, monetisation, acquisition, marketing budget.

The tone: blunt indie hacker, not a consultant in a suit. Rob Walling × Pieter Levels × Marc Lou. It calls things what they are: "this stinks", "shippable this weekend", "the gap is huge".

Invocation is simple:

```bash
/product-scout [niche or theme]
/product-scout [niche] type: [saas|mobile|extension|ai|all]
/product-scout [niche] audience: [b2b|b2c|creators|personal]
```

Examples:

- `/product-scout legal freelancers`
- `/product-scout parents of babies type: mobile`
- `/product-scout developer productivity type: extension`

### A closer look at idea #1: Kosa Lé (87/100 🔥)

> **Tagline:** "Learn, translate and speak Réunion Creole like a local."

**The pain it identified:**

> "I was born on Réunion but my parents only spoke French to me — when my grandmother speaks Creole, I understand about 60%. When I have to answer, I'm lost. Google Translate doesn't know Creole."

**The context it surfaced:**

- **Who:** Réunionnais aged 18-45 who partially understand Creole, 2nd/3rd generation diaspora, ~80K mainlanders living on the island
- **When:** family conversations (grandparents), local social media, coming back from a trip home
- **How many:** ~870K residents + ~250K French-speaking diaspora — estimated paying target: 30-50K
- **Why it isn't solved:** Réunion Creole has been an official regional language since 2014, but it's too small for Duolingo and too big for Google Translate. **Indie sweet spot.**

**The MVP it proposes:**

1. Conversational FR ↔ Creole translation on a dataset built from public lexicons and the regional language office
2. A "Konversasyon" mode: you speak French, the AI answers in Creole with local TTS audio
3. "5 min/day" themed lessons (cooking, family, market, *kabar*)

**Competitors identified:** Google Translate (no Creole support), web lexicons (static, ugly), Assimil (book + CD, old-school format), the regional language office (institutional, no app — *partnership possible*).

**Pricing:** Free 10 translations/day, Marmay plan €4.99/mo, Family plan €7.99/mo. 12-month target: 3-5K subscribers = **€15-25K MRR**.

**Three-channel acquisition plan:** daily local TikTok, diaspora Facebook groups, partnership with the language office plus local press.

That's the level of detail that changes things. Not "this could be interesting" — but "here's how you ship it".

## The three things it taught me

### 1. Honest scoring forces rigour

If I asked ChatGPT for "10 ideas about Réunion", I'd get 10 ideas with emojis and "this has real potential!". My skill drops Bazar Créole to **48/100** and says, verbatim, "niche is professional but small, thin margins, tech adoption hard with this audience".

That honesty is wired into the skill through:

- An explicit scoring grid (market size, pain intensity, solo feasibility, monetisation, competition, acquisition)
- One non-negotiable rule: *"If an idea stinks, say so. If the niche is saturated, say so and propose a pivot."*

That's the difference between a flattering brainstorm and a product audit.

### 2. A forced mix breaks the "SaaS only" bias

Left to itself, the model always returns web SaaS. It's in the training data.

So I wired this into the skill:

> *At least 2 mobile ideas, 2 web SaaS ideas, 1 extension/plugin/AI idea. The rest: free.*

The result: on the Réunion run I got 10 mobile apps (because I'd filtered explicitly), but in "all" mode the spread was far more useful. **A creative constraint beats a vague brief.**

### 3. It's a starting point, not an oracle

The report lands in 15 minutes with sourced quotes and real numbers — but it's an initial map, not scripture.

Before writing a line of Kosa Lé, I'd need to:

- Cross-check the quotes (talk to 10 Réunionnais who struggle with Creole)
- Validate a waitlist (simple landing page, target 200 emails in 7 days)
- Test the pricing (DM 20 target users: "what would you pay?")

<Callout tone="warn">
The scout doesn't replace validation. It gives you **10 well-formed hypotheses to validate**, instead of one vague idea. The trap is coding straight off the report — don't.
</Callout>
